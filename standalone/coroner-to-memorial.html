What happens, and roughly when

Typical ranges for an investigated death. Backlogs vary enormously between offices — ask yours for their current turnaround rather than trusting any of these numbers.

  • Hour 0–6 Body taken into coroner custody; next of kin notified. Nothing about the funeral can start until the body is released.
  • Hour 12–72 Agency press release, if there is one. Most departments withhold the name until next of kin are notified. You can ask the Public Information Officer to tell you before it goes out — they will usually agree, and it is far better than learning it from the news.
  • Day 0–3 Your formal statement to police. Ask whether you are a witness or a next-of-kin interview. Take someone with you. You may ask for a copy of your own statement afterwards.
  • Day 1–3 Autopsy performed. You can usually object on religious grounds — say so immediately, in writing, because the window is hours, not days.
  • Day 2–5 Body released to your funeral home. The funeral home collects it. You do not arrange transport yourself.
  • Day 3–10 Death certificate issued — cause often reads PENDING. This is normal for an investigated death and it is the single most expensive delay you will hit. See the warning under paying the funeral home.
  • Week 4–8 Toxicology returns. Twelve to sixteen weeks in a backlogged office is common. Toxicology is almost always what everything else is waiting on.
  • Week 6–16 Final autopsy report; death certificate amended with a cause. Request the amended certificate yourself — offices rarely send it unprompted. Insurers need this version.
  • Case close Clothing and held evidence released. Weeks, or many months if there is a prosecution. Other personal effects usually come back long before this.

Timings and rights described here follow United States practice — the Funeral Rule, the $255 Social Security payment and state next-of-kin hierarchies. Elsewhere the shape holds but the specifics will not. Nothing here is legal advice; when money or a dispute is involved, ask a probate attorney.
